The problem

AI can make studying easier. It can also make you stop thinking.

Most students already use AI. Very few were taught how. In the studies the book summarizes, the same kind of AI helped some students learn far more and left others worse off. The difference was how they used it.

Getting answers isn't the same as learning.

AI can sound confident — even when it's wrong.

Copying AI's work can leave you unprepared when you have to work alone.

Students need practical guidance on where AI helps — and where it should step back.

The solution isn't to avoid AI. It's to learn how to use it properly.

The Golden Rule

Use AI to think more, not less.

Handing work to a tool isn't automatically bad. The question is which work you hand over. Good offloading removes busywork. Bad offloading skips the very thinking you're meant to learn.

Bad AI use

AI does the thinking→Student copies→Short-term convenience→Weak learning

Good AI use

Student thinks→AI challenges or helps→Student checks→Student understands→Stronger learning

Struggle first, then ask.

The book's Exam Test: if AI vanished and you had to sit an exam on this tomorrow, would this use of AI have helped you prepare, or left you unprepared?

The prompt library

Don't just ask AI questions. Learn how to prompt it.

Learn the R-C-T-F-C formula — Role, Context, Task, Format, Constraints — then use ten ready-to-adapt starter prompts. Notice how many make you do the thinking.

1 · Socratic Tutor

Act as a Socratic tutor… one question at a time… hints if I'm stuck.

3 · Quiz Me

Using only my notes, write exam-style questions. Don't show answers yet.

5 · Solve Together

Don't solve it. Ask what I've tried, then give the next small hint.

7 · Feedback, Not Rewriting

Don't rewrite my draft. Give suggestions I can apply myself.

9 · Source Check

List the claims and what kind of source I should check for each.

Academic integrity

Use AI without compromising your own work.

Rules differ between schools, teachers, colleges and exam boards. Your institution's AI policy always comes first, including before this book.

1

Follow the policy

2

Verify

3

Disclose when required

AI can help with

  • Brainstorming
  • Explaining concepts
  • Practice questions
  • Feedback
  • Grammar and clarity, where permitted
  • Finding research leads

AI shouldn't replace

  • Your own thinking
  • Work you must complete independently
  • Sources you haven't checked yourself
  • Code or arguments you can't explain

Privacy & safety

Become a smarter AI user.

Protect private data

Never paste passwords, ID numbers, bank or medical details.

Check privacy settings

Know whether a tool uses your chats to train its models.

Verify facts and sources

Fake references can look real.

Watch for bias

Ask for multiple perspectives on people, culture and politics.

Doubt realistic fakes

Images, voices and videos can be generated. Find the original.

Keep working without AI

If you can't start a task without it, practice.

The pause test

Before you hit send: Is this allowed? Would I be comfortable if my teacher saw it? Am I giving away anything private?

School students, college students, and self-learners who want to use AI more effectively and responsibly.

No. The book emphasizes learning, verification, academic integrity and responsible use.
